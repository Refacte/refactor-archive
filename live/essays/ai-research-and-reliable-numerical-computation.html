AI already contributes proofs, organizing constructions, and connections in subjects once treated as a refuge for human conceptual work. I would concentrate specialist-AI research on reliable, economical adaptive sparse computation, where graph structure, numerical error, and execution cost jointly determine success. The strongest machine papers already address parts of this problem. The positive bet concerns cheaper, justified choices about retaining, repairing, or rebuilding a representation, and a larger specialist contribution to their discovery than on equally equipped structural-mathematics projects.

1. What the evidence changes

The old refuge in abstraction is over. I would no longer advise a mathematician to seek a durable human research advantage by moving further into algebraic geometry, representation theory, abstract algebra, or theoretical PDE. AI systems now contribute substantial proofs, useful organizing constructions, and connections between theories. The evidence reaches the conceptual work that was supposed to insulate these subjects. A presumption of human exclusivity at that level is no longer a credible basis for investing years of mathematical training. [1] [2] [3] [8] [16]

My positive bet favors specialist-agent teams working on adaptive sparse computation at the intersection of graph algorithms and numerical analysis. I would prioritize this work because useful existing methods leave recurring, consequential decisions about when to retain, repair, or rebuild a representation. Coefficients, arithmetic, hardware, and the remaining workload can change which decision pays. Finite graph and matrix operators make the representation explicit. Preservation inequalities connect its retained information to the required answer. Independent error checks and measurements of construction, application, checking, and recovery costs expose whether an improvement works. Repeated solves can repay the discovery of a reusable method.

The proposed specialist contribution is to obtain decisive information more economically than an agent's alternative investigation, then direct implementation and testing accordingly. The comparative wager is that this compact guidance produces a larger net increase in accepted results within budget than specialist guidance does on the structural projects chosen for comparison. The studies below document consequential interventions and strong automated comparators. The proposed experiment tests the comparative premium after full costs and shared methods are accounted for. [20] [48] [59] [97] [98] [101] [102] The experiment uses expertise already available to its participants. The cost and value of developing that expertise require a further account of training, shared resources and independent capability, developed in What Sustains Mathematical Expertise.

A four-vertex graph already exposes the issue. Exchanging its strong and weak edge weights can leave the matrix size, sparsity pattern, diagonal, and eigenvalues unchanged while defeating the old coarse space. The missing information is the alignment between the new operator and the directions that the approximation retains. The calculation below makes that failure explicit, then asks what an inexpensive certificate can establish before paying for a repair.

For a researcher choosing problems with measurable computational consequences, the recommendation rests on implemented gains as well as open questions. CAST's sampling changes and the studies of online solver configuration report reduced computational cost on their stated workloads. [48] [39] [62] These are gains from altering how a computation is represented or organized. They motivate collaboration in which mathematical diagnosis directs an agent's implementation and experiments toward changes that can pay for themselves. The further question is whether informative diagnoses and repairs can extend such gains affordably to fresh cases. Establishing a durable specialist advantage requires a further comparison with autonomous systems. The later experiment tests that claim after methods have been shared. The programme succeeds when it produces useful, justified methods at a worthwhile total cost, whoever supplies the decisive idea.

2. Conceptual work is already being automated

The Deligne-Drinfeld case turns on a choice of representation inside an abstract proof. The conjecture identifies the rational Grothendieck-Teichmüller Lie algebra with a free graded Lie algebra on one generator in each odd degree 3, 5, 7, and so on. Willwacher attributes the missing dimension bound to OpenAI and gives a specialist-led, AI-assisted reconstruction. The defining equations have integer coefficients. Reduction modulo two can enlarge their solution space, so an upper bound in characteristic two also bounds the rational space. Write A = x², B = y, and C = [x,y], then project P(A,B,C) to P(0,B,C). A representation of the four-strand braid Lie algebra turns the defining equations into an explicit reconstruction formula. The projection is therefore injective on the constrained solution space. Its image lies in Lie(C,[B,C]), giving the upper bound in weights at least three. Combined with the known rational lower bound, this closes the argument. [1]

P₃ = [A,B] + [B,C], P₃(0,B,C) = [B,C].

This weight-three example visibly discards a nonzero term. Recovery works because the original polynomial satisfies the defining relations. The reduction would lose information on arbitrary Lie polynomials. The proof thus constructs a compressed representation and establishes exactly why it suffices. That contribution occurs inside the machinery of associators and Lie algebras, at the conceptual level invoked to justify human insulation. [1]

Markarian's November note develops the example further. It interprets the projection geometrically through the double covering that sends z to z² and the filling of a puncture, and makes the reconstruction explicit. This geometric interpretation is Markarian's contribution. Human explanation can follow a proof architecture attributed to a machine and develop it into further structural mathematics. The former division of labour has become an unreliable guide to where people must contribute. [2]

Aletheia provides another kind of evidence. In Eigenweights for arithmetic Hirzebruch Proportionality, Tony Feng attributes the mathematical content to Aletheia and describes his role, apart from building the agent, as rewriting its output into a paper. The agent connected arithmetic eigenweights to symmetric-group representation theory through algebraic combinatorics and determined them for all classical groups. The accompanying report says the imported techniques were unfamiliar to the authors of the originating work. It also describes an independence-polynomial collaboration in which the agent supplied a broader roadmap and the human collaborators supplied the proofs. The report describes these autonomous milestones as relatively modest advances. They nevertheless establish useful cross-field organizing contributions. [3]

The eigenweights paper also reaches diagnosis. In type D with even rank, its calculation shows that a commutativity assumption needed for a result in the preceding work can fail. Two natural generators must be treated through their two-dimensional span rather than assumed to be individual eigenvectors. This is an example within structural mathematics of identifying the failed relationship and calculating in a representation that accommodates it. The paper attributes its mathematical content to Aletheia. [3]

OpenAI's larger catalogue is evidence of breadth, with a different evidential status. The repository snapshot checked on 4 November lists 721 manuscripts in 373 related families and approximately 42 percent of principal stated results formalized, from a broader evaluation posing approximately 4,000 problems. These are not 721 independent breakthroughs, and 42 percent is not a success rate. The repository calls the material raw research data at different verification stages. Its history records three withdrawals after a shared sign error and repairs to other manuscripts. An unexamined entry cannot carry the weight of an independently reconstructed proof. The catalogue should widen the investigation, not replace it. [4]

Modular representation theory supplies further examples of this breadth. OpenAI reports proofs of the blockwise Alperin weight conjecture for every finite group and prime, and of Donovan finiteness over fixed algebraically closed fields with bounded defect-group order. Central block-theoretic problems are now being attacked directly. These results are reported in preprints, and I have not located an independent specialist validation of either whole proof. The accompanying formalization records also cover the statement that rational Hodge classes on complex CM abelian varieties are algebraic, in every dimension and codimension, and specified Auslander-Reiten and Tachikawa counterexamples in homological algebra. These records do not establish that every surrounding conjecture is settled. Independent assessment would determine how far these reported proofs extend the reconstructed evidence above. [5] [6] [7]

The Saxl manuscript provides a second structural comparison. For the staircase partition ρ = (m,m−1,…,1), it tracks the cyclic module generated by the tensor product of its chosen row and column vectors. Coordinate projections separate a smaller staircase from a band. The proof must retain the actual generator and identify the projected image with the full induced module through explicit inverse maps. Mere occurrence of the desired constituents somewhere in the ambient tensor square would not supply the induction. A width-two band becomes a path contraction of 2 × 2 matrices, and dual-polytabloid pairings detect the required constituents. [99]

Even the constituent test needs a repair. In Example 5.4 an invertible segment product B = 12T, with T exchanging the two coordinates, has B₁₁ = 0. The fixed endpoint pairing consequently vanishes. Conjugating the dual test basis by the matrix Q with rows (1,1) and (1,2) makes the conjugated product’s (1,1) entry equal to 12 while preserving the original generator and endpoint letters. The final proof explicitly handles an uninformative test by changing the representation used to inspect the object. It supplies no chronological record of the model first failing and then discovering this repair. The formalization documentation reports the Saxl and universal-square positivity results, while excluding the stronger prescribed-tensor orbit-span assertion. [99]

First Proof supplies a more bounded test. Thirty expert reviewers assessed anonymized solutions to ten research problems known to have solutions of at most twelve pages. Systems had 24 hours without human input during testing. The third batch reports 7/10 correct for GPT-6 Astra and 9/10 for ProofCouncil, with reported ten-problem batch costs of $36 and $1,888 respectively. Here, correct means an average correctness score of at least 0.5 under the report's rubric, which includes minor, repairable errors. Those figures exclude the cost of creating and reviewing the benchmark and do not price the whole research process. They make it untenable to assume that obtaining a substantial research proof must require a human researcher to construct it. Exposition and attribution remained weaker than correctness. [8]

The June and October reports record lower reported batch costs alongside improved results. ProofCouncil rises from 7/10 to 9/10 correct, and the direct OpenAI entry from 5/10 to 7/10. The reports use fresh problem sets and changed testing conditions. They document successive capabilities without supplying a calibrated rate of progress. [8]

TheoPhysEval assesses expert-contributed theoretical-physics calculations using an automated final-answer checker. Under that checker, GPT-6 Astra with Codex answered 49 of 50 problems correctly with web access and 46 without web search or the supplied background papers. Computational tools remained available. The authors report that the answers were unpublished at evaluation, but some contributors had used AI and data leakage could not be ruled out. The results demonstrate substantial capability on technically demanding calculations under these conditions. [9]

3. A contribution has to earn its value

One response locates human value in research taste and problem selection. Caltech's October 2026 announcement of a mathematical AI platform presents Yisong Yue's view of research taste as a quality developed through human experience that AI currently lacks. The announcement also assigns connection-finding, conjecture suggestions, and possible strategies to the proposed AI system. Its proposed human contribution concerns judgment among research directions. Taste and problem selection must earn their value through what they improve in a completed investigation, including the cost of producing and checking that judgment. [91]

Wang and collaborators test whether models carry implicit hypotheses across mathematical framings. Across seven general-purpose model snapshots, 60.6 percent of abstracting rewrites on the held-out benchmark widen the asserted domain. A faithful translation has to retain the original scope, while a generalization has to justify changing it. The study's specified protocols and older models identify a failure to test in stronger systems. [10]

Human reconstruction of a machine proof remains mathematics. Detecting a lost hypothesis can be indispensable to a project. Those contributions establish their value through what they make possible. The same standard applies to numerical research. A difficult implementation or an interacting set of requirements creates a research problem, while the benefit of specialist participation still needs to be measured.

Nor can graph theory inherit the protection that abstraction is losing. Das and Fox credit GPT-5.6 Sol with the Murty-Simon edge bound floor(n²/4) and the even-order equality case. The authors simplified the argument and supplied equality for odd order too. Oum's earlier exposition develops OpenAI's cycle-double-cover proof. Nagda, Raghavan, and Thakurta use AlphaEvolve to find combinatorial objects and reduction gadgets. Xu's cubic counterexample to the general Bilu-Linial signing conjecture and Lin-Zhou's revised spectral-signing work also report model contributions. The restricted Ramanujan-base question and efficient construction questions must be distinguished from the statements already addressed. [11] [12] [13] [14] [15]

The same change reaches theoretical PDE. In First Proof's Darcy inverse problem, ProofCouncil removed the logarithmic loss in the human upper bound, with all three reviewers finding the mathematics essentially correct. OpenAI's October local-smoothing release asserts the Euclidean wave estimate in every spatial dimension at least two, with accompanying formalization records and related restriction and Kakeya statements. The former has expert benchmark review. The latter remains an attributed research release whose mathematical claims need independent assessment. Neither supports the assumption that sophisticated PDE analysis is inherently protected. [8] [16]

Crouzeix's conjecture brings the warning into operator theory and numerical analysis. Townsend and Greenbaum report that they and Crouzeix checked Jin's AI-assisted proof and believe it correct. The decisive theorem emerged from an approximately sixteen-hour model run. Turning such a theorem into an economical numerical method is a further task, but it cannot be reserved for humans merely by calling it implementation. [17]

Subtle algorithm design is exposed too. Alman and Vassilevska Williams credit Claude with an underlying algorithm that they understood, simplified, strengthened, and extended. For polynomially bounded integer inputs, their stated bounds include O(n1.9992) for 3SUM and O(n2.9995) for directed all-pairs shortest paths with polynomially bounded integer weights and no negative cycles. The exponents do not establish practical speedups. The contribution nevertheless rules out an easy fallback to 'machines cannot invent the subtle algorithm.' [18]

The OpenAI collection itself reaches the constructive programme. Its strong-thin-tree manuscript claims a deterministic polynomial-bit construction controlling every cut of an edge-connected multigraph. The construction uses rational approximation, exact covariance completion, matrix certificates, and accumulated-loss control through successive sparsifications. Its cut guarantee must be distinguished from two-sided spectral approximation of the original Laplacian. The accompanying matching manuscript claims almost-linear input-size time in a randomized word-RAM model, including unsuccessful random paths, multiword arithmetic, allocation, data movement, and output. Its changing sparse representations and exact dyadic updates are part of the cost analysis. These reported results directly challenge a division that reserves representation-aware, precision-aware algorithm design for specialists. [97] [98]

There are also explicit limits to the advertised conclusions. The Fourier-transform manuscript makes no numerical-stability claim and supplies no useful crossover estimate. The matrix-multiplication family states an arithmetic-exponent improvement without a competitive finite matrix size. Those limits identify further numerical and computational questions. The thin-tree and matching claims show why the release as a whole must also be examined for constructions that already account for precision and representation maintenance. A specialist comparison should give agents access to those methods before testing fresh problems. [56] [97] [98] [100]

SciPiVot supplies limited direct evidence about research decisions. It reconstructs 248 decisions from 26 scientific projects. Fresh PhD students and postdocs, with AI assistance available, selected the supported next step in 73.4 percent of cases, compared with 63.5 percent for the best tested model with access to the project record. Both candidate plans were supplied, and the cases were selected retrospectively from human corrections. This measures recognition of a better-supported correction and provides neither a matched-cost comparison nor a sparse-solver subgroup. It motivates testing researcher-led diagnosis under conditions that include its production cost. [61]

Complete structural research also includes choosing definitions, connecting theories, finding obstructions, and deciding which formulation makes a problem tractable. A fair comparison of specialties would include all that work. The numerical direction proposed here needs its own affirmative case. That case begins with the computational value of a representation, including representations discovered by machines.

4. Why compression still matters

One important use of abstraction is to replace many difficult calculations with a compact method that can be reused. In his 1984 essay on the computer revolution, republished in 2016, Michael Atiyah argued that theoretical developments had often evolved to avoid large calculations because humans were poor at them. He also worried that computers, being good at calculation, would have less incentive to seek conceptual alternatives. His account identifies an important motive for theory. His prediction about machines is a further claim, open to evidence. [81]

Algebra already shows how a framework can become computational equipment. Wilf-Zeilberger theory supplies certificate forms and verification arguments for suitable hypergeometric identities. Expressing an identity in the appropriate class permits a computer to search for a certificate and check its identity and boundary conditions. The framework makes a family of subsequent problems more systematic. Its intellectual value survives the automation of its use. [82]

Machines also have reasons to seek compact structure. FunSearch searches for programs that construct mathematical objects. Its authors report that generated programs revealed parsimonious symmetries and supported further investigation. AlphaEvolve searches over candidate objects, constructor programs, and search algorithms, with different representations working better in different settings. A program that generates a useful family or narrows a search can repay the effort spent discovering it. [83] [44]

Human manageability and computational economy are different benefits. AI can retrieve calculations and suggest constructions, reducing what a researcher must recall unaided. Executing, storing, communicating, and checking computations still consume resources. A representation remains useful when it reduces that work while preserving the information required by the task.

A representation can preserve the relevant information exactly. In the Deligne-Drinfeld argument, reconstruction establishes that a projection retains the constrained solutions. My work on Taiko product structures translates a specified group-ring construction into finite incidence geometry, where pressure and intersection constraints expose an obstruction missed by coarse counts. Sparse elimination begins with an exact energy identity and then permits approximation. In each case the mathematical obligation is to justify the passage back to the original problem. [1] [80] [58]

Compare the strongest constructions

A comparison at the level of proof mechanisms rules out an easy separation. Deligne-Drinfeld makes an apparently lossy projection recoverable on its solution space. Saxl preserves a specific generator through successive reductions and repairs a failed pairing. Both require a bridge from the reduced representation back to the original claim. The selected graph-algorithm manuscripts add explicit approximation and resource bounds to their preservation arguments. [1] [97] [98] [99]

The strong-thin-tree construction must control cuts while retaining enough rank information to extract a spanning tree. It combines an edge's normalized incidence vector and a second, rank-preserving vector in a stacked frame. The signing routine requires rational input whose covariance is exactly the identity. Approximate whitening alone would fail that interface. The manuscript repairs it with rational approximation and an exact decomposition of the residual covariance, adding padding vectors before signing. Rational positive-semidefiniteness tests certify the result, and the proof transfers the estimate back after discarding the padding. Separate estimates control cut loads and surviving tree packings. Their accumulated losses yield the claimed C/k cut-thinness bound in polynomial bit complexity. This already joins representation choice, interface repair, approximation, and a global guarantee. [97]

The constants expose a further question within that achievement. The displayed signing estimate permits Cs = 2²⁰ and error εr = Cs r−1/8, where r is the current guaranteed tree-packing parameter. The coefficient in its lower bound for the next packing becomes positive only when r > 2¹⁶⁸. Positivity alone still does not ensure that the integer bound retains a tree. The proof uses a sufficiently large absolute cutoff and returns an arbitrary spanning tree below it, absorbing that regime into the final constant. This illustrates how conservative the sufficient guarantee is. It supplies neither a necessary input size nor a measured performance crossover. Obtaining useful finite-instance constants remains a further mathematical task. The cut guarantee also differs from the two-sided spectral approximation needed for the preconditioning applications below. [97]

The matching manuscript is an even closer challenge to a protected diagnostic role. Its local projection procedure explores an amount of represented incidence and bin weight bounded by the available debt, so a progress account pays for obtaining corrective information. Its engine retains exact physical edge values while changing sparse forest representations. Deleting a copy transfers its accumulated increment to a base value, and retained copies keep their histories. Separate counters record absolute motion before cancellation, flag cached values that need refreshing, and pay for the refresh cascade. Exact dyadic bookkeeping supplies the precision interface. The final schedule charges allocation, multiword arithmetic, unsuccessful paths, and output, and chooses among parameter branches by instruction-level dovetailing. Its claimed almost-linear bound is asymptotic, with no competitive finite-size crossover established here. It nevertheless already contains automated detection, state-preserving repair, and cost control. [98]

These comparisons distinguish what a result establishes from the further deliverable a project may seek. The reconstructed structural theorem settles its precise statement through its recovery argument. The constructive graph papers add resource-bounded procedures within their stated models. Reliable numerical deployment can additionally require useful constants, attainable accuracy, and an advantage at the actual workload size. The Fourier-transform manuscript makes this last boundary explicit by leaving stability and useful crossover outside its exact-arithmetic claim. Structural projects also have unfinished obligations in proof checking, explanation, generalization, and computational realization. The comparison supplies no ordering of the human contribution to those tasks. [1] [56] [97] [98] [99]

Why I would prioritize adaptive sparse computation

Compression, diagnosis, and reuse alone do not distinguish the proposed numerical work from structural mathematics. Roune and Stillman's Gröbner-basis algorithms use signatures and syzygies to discard unnecessary work. Their S-pair representation reduces the number of stored monomials from potentially quadratic to linear, while retaining an integer for each queued pair, and different ideals expose different computational bottlenecks. [93] Kevin Buzzard's participant account of the Liquid Tensor Experiment describes a complementary case. Clarifying what was actually needed allowed Commelin and Scholze to replace stable-homotopy machinery with a weaker sufficient result. The pressure came from the cost of formalization. Structural mathematics already contains the intellectual mechanisms on which the positive thesis relies. [94]

The stronger reason for choosing the numerical programme is the conjunction of accuracy, economical representation, and actual execution. These requirements can reopen an established problem. Changed coefficients can alter which error modes matter. A communication-saving reformulation can amplify rounding errors. A shorter workload can make a useful repair too expensive. The examples below exhibit each mechanism. They give a collaboration recurring, concrete questions about what must be preserved and what can be afforded. [20] [48] [62] [95]

CAST makes the unresolved decision unusually specific. Increasing its sampling parameter improves a normalized local second-moment bound, yet that bound does not price the fill created by later eliminations. Extra contributions stay cheap on the low-degree Spielman family. They can propagate expensively on Chimera systems. High-degree pivots in the Sachdeva family behave differently because they occur within already dense blocks. Pivot degree alone therefore does not settle whether additional sampling pays. A useful controller would predict enough of that downstream effect to choose a configuration more cheaply than constructing all candidate factors. Its decision must also account for how many solves remain. The detailed performance comparison below gives this mathematical prediction problem an observed computational consequence. [48]

The coarse-space literature exposes a complementary obstacle. An operator-weighted discrepancy can identify consequential approximation error more accurately than a geometric comparison of subspaces, yet evaluating the sharper diagnostic can itself require solves with the original operator. The cited work uses it offline. An affordable online substitute needs both a justified connection to the missed error and an accounting of the work saved. When those diagnostic solves cannot be repaid over the remaining workload, the online selection problem remains open. [86]

The cost of execution survives the automation of mathematical reasoning. Ballard, Demmel, Holtz, and Schwartz prove communication lower bounds for specified classes of dense and sparse linear-algebra algorithms, including factorizations and compositions of operations. In their model, moving data between memory levels or processors is an additional resource, and minimizing arithmetic alone can leave that cost high. The assumptions concern the executed algorithm and its memory access. Better recall of previous calculations cannot remove those costs while retaining the same execution. A new representation or organization can change them. [96]

Graph theory supplies elimination orders, sparse supports, partitions, and transfer operators. Numerical analysis connects these choices to energy, convergence, rounding error, and the final answer. I would concentrate on the intersection where an established method leaves an unresolved decision under changed conditions. The target is a useful conjunction of guarantees, construction cost, and execution cost on a declared workload. A proof with conservative constants, a fast method with incomplete global control, and an informative but expensive diagnostic each leave different parts of that conjunction open. The thin-tree, CAST, and coarse-space examples concern different mathematical objects. Their common lesson is the need to identify the missing obligation precisely before proposing a repair. [48] [86] [97]

This supports a positive research preference with a concrete product. Develop reusable tests, bounds, and adaptive constructions that close those gaps at a cost the workload repays. The evidence for this preference includes implemented improvements, recurrent failure mechanisms, and explicit limitations of available guarantees. It survives even when a machine supplies the decisive idea. Abstract sophistication offers no corresponding protection from the demonstrated structural capabilities. Computational algebra can enter the same positive programme when it faces comparable representation and execution constraints. [1] [20] [48] [62] [86] [93] [95]

The stronger centaur prediction concerns who discovers the useful control most economically. Similar stagnation can reflect a missing coarse direction, an inaccurate inner solve, or drift between the computed and true residual. Those explanations call for different tests and repairs. Trying several full alternatives can spend much of the research budget. A specialist adds value by identifying a cheaper discriminating observation or proving a condition that makes an expensive trial unnecessary. The cost of obtaining that insight, and the consequences of wrong diagnoses, belong in the comparison. Existing automatic residual-replacement rules and the matching construction already perform versions of this task. Every group must receive that equipment before a further specialist premium is credited. [20] [63] [95] [98]

Measuring what specialist guidance adds

The comparative claim needs a further step. Fix the accuracy, reliability, and worthwhile cost-improvement requirements. Let CA be the total charged research cost at which an autonomous investigation first produces an accepted method, and CH the corresponding cost for a specialist-agent team. Assign infinite cost to a run that produces no qualifying result within its declared evaluation protocol. CH includes obtaining and using the guidance, checking it, and pursuing any wrong starts. At a research budget b, the specialist premium is the difference below. This is the primary discovery outcome of Section 9 written explicitly.

Δ(b) = Pr(CH ≤ b) - Pr(CA ≤ b).

The numerical comparison requires this difference to be larger on the declared numerical portfolio than on the structural portfolio, under the agreed budget accounting and independently assessed deliverables. A large cost of choosing the wrong solver establishes the possible value of a better decision. It does not establish that specialist guidance improves the decision. Even positive average time savings need not increase success at the chosen budget if they arise on easy cases while guidance consumes the budget on difficult ones. The effect must appear in accepted results within budget.

Complementarity research gives this comparison a useful framework. Hemmer and colleagues distinguish potential gains from gains actually realized, identifying differences in information and capabilities as sources of joint improvement. In the October 2026 AutoAnalysis preprint, division of labor is associated with better collaboration outcomes. Across the subset of experiments that also evaluated AI alone, combined performance falls below the better-performing standalone condition on average. Those moderator associations are exploratory and noncausal. The proposed numerical division of work must therefore exploit a particular advantage in obtaining or using information, with coordination and errors charged. Task difficulty and human oversight alone do not establish that advantage. Structural projects can offer it too. [103] [104]

The proposed division of work is nevertheless concrete. A specialist supplies a short discriminating test, preservation condition, or prediction about where fill or rounding error will become consequential. An agent performs the larger implementation and experimental investigation that follows. CAST, the coarse-space analysis, and the residual-gap examples identify places where such a compact intervention could redirect substantial work. A structural specialist can similarly supply a decisive blueprint, missing hypothesis, or imported lemma. The Archon comparison demonstrates why that contribution must be included on the structural side. [48] [63] [86] [90] [95]

  • Consequential uncertainty must recur. The numerical literature documents changes in operators, arithmetic, and workloads that reopen decisions with measured computational consequences. [20] [48] [62] [95]
  • Specialists must resolve enough of that uncertainty more economically than equipped agents. The numerical papers establish candidate decisions and repairs. The relative cost and reliability of obtaining the decisive information remain to be measured.
  • Further specialist contributions must repay their cost after successful rules have been shared. Recurring problems also create opportunities to automate diagnosis, so recurrence alone supplies no lasting human premium.

The scientific-software cases below add observations of specialist redirection and faster assisted artifacts. Their incomplete controls and cost records make them starting points for the proposed comparison. They identify interventions whose production cost, transfer to fresh cases, and benefit over equipped agents can be measured. Together with the numerical constructions, they give the proposed division of work concrete cases on which to succeed or fail. [101] [102]

My comparative bet is strongest for selected numerical projects with changed operators or execution, ambiguous cheap diagnostics, and expensive alternatives. I expect specialist participation there to improve the probability of a worthwhile result at fixed research cost more than in the structural projects selected for comparison. The structural comparator must include capable teams doing complete projects, including formulation, representation choices, proof construction, and infrastructure. Comparing an unfinished numerical deployment with an already completed structural theorem would prejudge the result. The papers establish why the numerical decisions recur and matter. They do not establish that a human obtains the decisive information more cheaply than an equally equipped agent. Structural projects can satisfy the same conditions. The proposed experiment must therefore compare the incidence, reliability, and full cost of successful interventions in declared portfolios. Final papers record mathematical products, while this claim concerns the research processes that produced them.

Successful diagnostics should become shared equipment. After adding them to every group's tools, test fresh cases again. This gives the programme a cumulative mathematical product and makes the proposed specialist advantage answerable to improving agents. The numerical research preference rests on the concrete gaps above. A larger comparative human premium is its demanding, testable prediction.

For sparse computation, the resulting question is precise. How much information can be discarded while the retained representation controls the required answer at an affordable cost? The finite examples below show what such preservation requires.

5. What a sparse representation must preserve

Start with a finite weighted graph. Orient its edges, let B be the edge-vertex incidence matrix with -1 at each edge's tail and +1 at its head, and put its positive edge weights in the diagonal matrix W. The graph Laplacian L has the following quadratic form.

L = BTWB,   xTLx = Σedges {i,j} wij(xi - xj)2.

A constant vector has zero energy. For a connected graph this is the entire kernel. One can fix a vertex potential or work on the mean-zero subspace to obtain a positive-definite problem. A related class consists of symmetric diagonally dominant matrices with nonpositive off-diagonal entries, abbreviated SDDM here. Such a matrix has the form A = L + Γ, where Γ is nonnegative diagonal. It is positive definite when every connected component has a positive grounding term. The scope here is this concrete matrix class. [19] [26]

Elimination makes the central difficulty visible. Suppose a vertex is joined to three neighbors by edges of weight one. For fixed neighbor values, minimize the energy over the central value. The minimizer is their average. Substitution leaves exactly the energy of a triangle on the three neighbors, each new edge having weight one third. For a star with positive incident-weight vector a, elimination gives the Schur complement below.

S = diag(a) - aaT/s,   s = Σiai.
For a = (1,1,1)T, every new edge has weight 1/3.

Elimination preserves minimized energy exactly. Repeated elimination also couples previously unconnected variables, creating fill. Retaining every new interaction can destroy the economy of a sparse input. Discarding interactions changes the energy that made the elimination correct. Spectral sparsification seeks a much smaller graph whose energy approximates the original for every vector. Effective-resistance sampling assigns importance using the surrounding network as well as the edge weight. [19]

A sharp sparsification theorem shows how quantitative this compression can be. Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava prove that, for 0 < ε < 1, a weighted graph on n vertices has a reweighted subgraph with at most ceil((n - 1)/ε²) edges whose energy lies between (1 - ε)² and (1 + ε)² times the original energy for every vector. The guarantee controls an entire family of calculations. Obtaining such a representation cheaply and using it in an efficient solver are additional algorithmic requirements. [84]

A preconditioned iteration repeatedly applies an inexpensive approximation to the inverse, aiming to reduce the total work needed to solve the original system. Write that action as solving with an auxiliary matrix M. For symmetric positive-definite A and M, suppose positive constants α and β satisfy the following inequalities for every vector x.

α xTMx ≤ xTAx ≤ β xTMx.

Then the generalized eigenvalues of A relative to M lie between α and β, and the corresponding condition number is at most β/α. For a fixed positive-definite preconditioner in exact arithmetic, this ratio controls the standard worst-case convergence bound for conjugate gradients. A smaller condition number alone need not produce fewer iterations on a particular problem, since the spectral distribution matters. The computational benefit depends on convergence together with the cost of constructing and applying M. For Laplacians the comparison is made on the orthogonal complement of their common kernel. [52] [75]

Multigrid approaches the loss of information in another way. A cheap relaxation reduces some error components, and a coarse correction represents those that remain. Algebraically smooth error is error that the chosen relaxation struggles to remove. Its meaning depends on the operator and relaxation. Strongly connected regions joined by weak edges can move almost independently at low energy. A coarse representation must account for the resulting modes. The relevant objects are vectors, matrix blocks, graph partitions, and transfer operators. [20]

Accuracy has a similar trap. In the following exact two-variable system, the second variable is weakly weighted.

A = diag(1, 10-8),   b = (1, 10-8)T,
x = (1,1)T,   y = (1,0)T.

The relative residual in the infinity norm is 10-8, while the relative forward error in the same norm is 1. The proposed answer loses the entire second component. Interpreting a residual therefore requires conditioning information and a specified error measure. LAPACK's error analysis makes that distinction explicit. [21]

A more discriminating inexpensive observation exposes this particular failure. With r = b - A y and absolute values taken entrywise, the componentwise backward error is

ωc = maxi |ri| / (|A| |y| + |b|)i = 1.

Its denominator is (2, 10-8), so the lost second component remains visible. Computing this quantity needs a sparse matrix-vector product, without an inverse. Forward-error control still requires the appropriate conditioning information. The example shows why choosing the right observable can be more useful than refining an aggregate residual. [21]

A spectral comparison holding for every vector can support every compatible right-hand side for the same operator. Its construction and justification can therefore serve many solves. The research problem is to obtain this control with a representation cheap enough to construct and apply, or to find affordable observations that justify retaining or repairing it after the operator changes. Approximate elimination and coarse spaces expose different versions of this problem. [20] [52] [58]

6. Constructing, diagnosing, and repairing

For Laplacians and positive-definite SDDM matrices, two concrete targets are sparsifying the interactions created by elimination and representing the error left by a cheap relaxation. Both require control of the resulting operator alongside the cost of constructing and applying it.

The choices often concern elimination orders, sampled spanning trees, coarse vertices, aggregates, and sparse connection patterns. Matrix analysis supplies the criterion those choices must meet. A connected sample or an economical partition is useful only if the resulting operator retains the required quantitative control. The graph construction and the numerical guarantee are parts of the same problem. [20] [47]

The Simons workshop report states related research problems. It asks for parameterized matrix families suitable for both practical evaluation and theoretical analysis, together with algorithms that are fast in both settings. Its multigrid problem combines an implicit inverse with uniform spectral control, near-linear construction, and linear application cost for a polynomially bounded subclass of the grounded SDDM matrices above. Its nonnormal-solver questions seek bounds describing the early behaviour of GMRES. These targets require the guarantee, its construction cost, and its predictive value to be considered together. [75]

Approximate elimination and accumulated error

Gao, Kyng, and Spielman's AC(k) work attaches a precise mathematical question to an implemented solver. Its approximate-Cholesky construction sparsifies the interactions created during elimination while maintaining connected sampled support among a removed variable's neighbors. The parameter controls the sampling effort. The experiments show favorable robustness and performance on broad SDDM workloads, alongside cases where established competitors are faster. These are existing effective solvers, not evidence that the matrix class has remained unsolved. [26]

The economical AC(2) construction still lacks the global preconditioning guarantee the authors seek. Section 6 conjectures a relative condition number O(log2 m), possibly O(log m), with high probability, where m is the input edge count. The construction has proved local and structural properties, but those properties do not supply the conjectured spectral bound. The question is how random distortions accumulate through a sequence of changing Schur complements. [26]

Related algorithms show how local accuracy can control a complete solve. Kyng, Lee, Peng, Sachdeva, and Spielman analyze Schur-complement chains in which the global spectral distortion is bounded by twice the sum of the local error tolerances, in their exponential comparison convention, while application work is charged across the levels. The chain must control both the approximate Schur complements and the inverses used for eliminated blocks. The result applies to a different construction under different assumptions. It makes error allocation precise by relating local tolerances to a global accuracy budget and the work charged across levels. [58]

A useful project could prove a restricted guarantee, find an obstruction to a proposed argument, or identify which graph structures require more sampling. A diagnosis should distinguish a deficient sampling rule from a poor ordering or a tolerance that is unnecessarily strict. Its repair might increase work locally while reducing fill or iterations elsewhere. The proof problem and the computational problem inform one another.

Why the obvious invariants are insufficient

An elementary example isolates the spectral obligation. Let L be the Laplacian of a triangle whose three edges have weight one. Choose an edge uniformly at random, give it weight 3 - 2 ε, and give the other two edges weight ε, with 0 < ε < 1. Every sampled graph remains connected and has the same constant-vector kernel. Each edge's expected weight is one, so the sampled Laplacian Ls has the stated expectation.

E[Ls] = L,
κ(Ls relative to L on the mean-zero subspace) = (2 - ε)/ε.

To check the second claim, suppose the heavy edge joins vertices 1 and 2. The vectors (1,1,-2) and (1,-1,0) are orthogonal eigenvectors of both L and Ls. Their generalized eigenvalues for Ls relative to L are ε and 2 - ε, respectively, and they span the mean-zero subspace. The relative condition number is therefore (2 - ε)/ε and becomes arbitrarily large. Connectivity and unbiasedness survive while spectral quality deteriorates. This is an original illustrative calculation, not a counterexample to AC(2), VAC, or CAST.

Recent constructions already go well beyond those two elementary properties. Baumann, Kyng, and Zöcklein's VAC uses spanning-tree sampling to replace the clique arising at an elimination step, preserving the desired marginals and connectivity with efficient sequential and parallel sampling. Chaitanya, Musco, and Gionis's CAST develops a related tree construction, a local optimality characterization, and a tunable bound on the normalized local second moment. CAST explicitly separates these local guarantees from a worst-case spectral theorem for the complete factorization. Unbiasedness and connected support alone are therefore not new contributions. A new sampler must improve the construction, its cost, or a quantitative guarantee. Controlling accumulated distortion is a particularly direct target. [47] [48]

CAST supplies a concrete example. Compared with AC, CAST-1 changes the local sampling rule while retaining the ordering, factor representation, and PCG path. Its mean per-system speedup ratios are 1.17 on 17 SuiteSparse matrices and 1.18 on 128 Chimera-IPM systems, with each factor reused for 250 right-hand sides. The relative performance of CAST-2 and AC(2) reverses across graph families. The authors connect that reversal to whether additional edges propagate through later elimination steps. Their CAST-1 elimination profile helps explain the regimes on the tested corpus, but obtaining it requires a first factorization, and they do not test it as an adaptive selection rule. A cheaper prediction of when additional sampling will repay its construction and fill costs could guide an adaptive method. A proposed rule must beat fixed settings and existing automatic selectors on unseen families after its own observation and setup costs are charged to the declared workload. [48]

Retaining the difficult modes on a coarse level

For a symmetric positive-definite matrix A, let the columns of a full-column-rank matrix P describe coarse corrections. The standard coarse matrix defines the exact coarse correction below.

Ac = PTAP,
xnew = x + PAc-1PT(b - Ax).

For the error e = x_exact - x, the coarse correction alone has the error-propagation operator below. If PT A v = 0, then E_c v = v; if v lies in the column space of P, then E_c v = 0. The surrounding smoother must reduce the errors that this projection leaves behind.

enew = Ece,
Ec = I - P(PTAP)-1PTA.

This is finite-dimensional linear algebra. The hard design question is which columns and entries P needs. Global spectral information can identify useful coarse directions, but computing it may already be expensive. A dense P can also make PT A P costly to form and solve. Local matrix information and graph aggregation offer cheaper approximations. Their adequacy depends on the relaxation they accompany and the modes they retain. A two-level result with an exact coarse solve is not automatically a theorem about a recursively applied multilevel implementation. [20]

The literature supplies concrete constructions and limitations. Constrained local approximate ideal restriction combines local transfer construction with constraints on selected modes and uses aggressive coarsening to keep hierarchies economical. Optimal-transfer theory characterizes best choices for wider nonsymmetric and indefinite settings. Sparse and inexpensive realization requires additional work. Algebraic Schwarz methods construct coarse information from local generalized eigenproblems. In Al Daas, Jolivet, Nataf, and Tournier's published analysis, the theorem concerns SPD matrices and its bound is not fully independent of the number of subdomains. Broader matrix classes appear in experiments. These distinctions identify the assumptions and costs a new construction would have to address. [49] [50] [51]

A bounded project can fix one matrix family and one baseline, then ask whether a smaller support, cheaper local eigensolve, or different partition preserves the relevant convergence behavior at lower total cost. If it cannot, a small counterexample may identify the missing mode and suggest a repair. These mechanisms already have automatic implementations: adaptive smoothed aggregation generates missing near-nullspace candidates, compatible relaxation helps assess coarse choices, and hierarchy-reuse methods update changing systems without repeating every setup operation. A new result must improve on these methods, not simply rename what they already do. [54] [76] [78] [79]

Adaptation can also take place during a solve. Huh, Cartis, and Nakatsukasa's APLICUR starts from a small sketch and incrementally refines a CUR-based preconditioner, interleaving updates with LSQR phases. Its printed analysis needs a target restriction absent from the stated assumptions. The lower singular-value and condition-number bounds can fail even with an exact CUR approximation. Adding a target at least as large as the regularization parameter repairs these bounds, and the regularized default target satisfies that condition. Note 87 gives an exact counterexample and the corrected scope. The experiments examine when additional spectral correction repays its cost. This makes the timing of representation improvement an algorithmic variable. [87]

A changed graph can defeat an unchanged coarse space

Consider the four-cycle 1-2-3-4-1 with A = L + ε I, where 0 < ε < 1. Initially give edges 12 and 34 weight one, and edges 23 and 41 weight ε. Let P contain the indicator vectors of the two strong pairs, {1,2} and {3,4}. Now interchange the strong and weak weights and call the changed matrix A'. It has the same sparsity pattern, diagonal, and eigenvalues as A because a relabeling relates the two graphs. But the old coarse space has become misaligned with the strong connections. The diagram shows the retained aggregates and the signs of v = (1,-1,-1,1)T. Each old aggregate contains opposite signs, while the new strong pairs each have one sign.

A′v = 3εv,   PTA′v = 0.

The exact coarse correction therefore leaves v untouched. The diagonal of A' is (1 + 2 ε) I, so a weighted Jacobi step multiplies this error by 1 - 3 ω ε/(1 + 2 ε). For a fixed admissible relaxation weight, that factor approaches one as ε decreases. Changing the aggregates to {1,4} and {2,3} puts v in the coarse space, and the exact coarse correction removes it. This is an elementary illustrative calculation, not a new multigrid result or a test of AI capability.

The example identifies the missing information. Matrix size, sparsity, diagonal, and even the spectrum alone do not describe the relation between the current operator and the retained coarse directions. On a large graph, the research question is whether changed coefficients and observed smoothing behaviour can reveal this mismatch early enough to improve on existing reuse and enrichment policies after observation cost is charged. A useful rule could choose among reuse, different relaxation, local enrichment, and rebuilding. Automatic competitors must be allowed the same choices. The case matters especially when only one or a few solves remain, leaving little opportunity to recover the cost of unsuccessful experiments. This is a concrete test of whether a description retains what the question requires.

Checking a retained preconditioner

Some changes admit an inexpensive certificate. Let A = L(w) + μ I and A' = L(w') + μ I on the same graph, with positive edge weights and fixed μ > 0. Choose 0 < c ≤ 1 ≤ C so that every edge's new weight divided by its old weight lies between c and C. Comparing the edge energies and the common grounding term gives the first inequality below. For a fixed symmetric positive-definite preconditioner M with an already proved comparison α M ≤ A ≤ β M, transitivity gives the second.

cA ≤ A′ ≤ CA,
cαM ≤ A′ ≤ CβM.

This is an elementary consequence of the energy identity. In exact arithmetic, one pass through the edge weights computes admissible c and C. The unchanged preconditioner M then has a proved relative condition-number bound C β/(c α) for A'. Comparing this bound with a declared condition-number limit checks the spectral requirement for reuse. Timing and setup costs determine whether reuse is economical. The bound can be conservative when global weight ratios obscure where changes occur. Sharper control would need to exploit more graph structure while retaining a worthwhile cost. A floating-point certificate also needs rounding control for these constants. Retaining transfer operators while updating level matrices or smoothers changes M and requires additional analysis. The pilot below tests these two policies separately. [19] [52]

From matrix solvers to PDE applications

Differential equations supply important sparse matrices, but the initial solver problem can be specified by the matrix, the right-hand side, and the required error measure. Graph incidence and finite matrix inequalities are enough to state it. Sobolev spaces and homological language are not universal prerequisites for this programme. A claim of accuracy for a continuous PDE also needs a valid approximation argument. For smooth finite-difference problems this can use Taylor expansion and a discrete maximum principle. More general settings benefit from other tools. [53]

Compatible vector discretizations provide a demanding later application. Their edge and face incidences constrain the transfers, and their coefficient weights determine the energies. Shen and collaborators' three-dimensional H(curl) construction leaves a sparse multilevel realization open. Tuminaro and Glusa supply a different sparse construction with commuting constraints. The two papers vary coefficients in different terms, so one does not settle the other's robustness question. Auxiliary-space and GenEO methods remain substantive alternatives. Finite element exterior calculus explains a broad route from continuous structure to stable discretization, but that route need not organize the finite solver research itself. [22] [23] [24] [25] [43]

A documented change of representation

Nguyen's CLAX-PT study of cosmological software documents a related intervention. Over 33 sessions, an agent adjusted coefficients in an analytic one-loop multipole projection that omitted the required angular dependence of the baryon-acoustic-oscillation damping. Errors remained 8-86 percent. A generic request to reconsider the architecture failed. A physicist identified the missing angular dependence and proposed Gauss-Legendre quadrature. The agent implemented the redesign in one session, bringing four of the six affected outputs below one percent and leaving the remaining two at about 1-2 percent in the reported results. [101]

The repair changed what the computation could represent. A separate intervention rejected a fitted scalar that passed existing tests but lacked a physical derivation. The account is one partly retrospective project with unrecoverable inference costs and no matched autonomous continuation. It demonstrates a consequential specialist intervention. Its frequency, net cost, and persistence remain to be tested. [101]

What a useful method must deliver

  • A justified representation. Relate its retained interactions or directions to the original system and the required error measure.
  • An informative diagnosis. Distinguish inadequate representation from ineffective relaxation, poor scaling, arithmetic error, or excessive construction cost.
  • An effective repair. Explain which modification addresses the diagnosed failure and under which conditions it preserves the required property.
  • A transferable improvement. Test fresh instances and count the probes, rejected attempts, rebuilding, checking, and recovery needed to obtain the result.

7. Agents already take part in this programme

Automated diagnosis and method discovery already contribute to this programme. Their successes also set a baseline for any claim about what a specialist adds.

Coding, benchmarking, and revising a solver are already being automated. Bader and collaborators report agents improving the tuned VieCut minimum-cut implementation by factors of 1.28 sequentially and 1.63 at 32 threads on the real-world k-core workload. All 23 accepted edits received human review, and the same 53-instance workload was used for threshold tuning and reported timing. This is productive algorithm engineering under the stated conditions. [28]

LinearSolveBench brings the challenge closer to the proposed specialty. Its released C/HYPRE solvers were selected from 640 candidates per model using 96 magnetic-diffusion development cases. The best reported result is about a 1.85-fold speedup over GMRES(50) with BoomerAMG in the original measurements, with all 96 cases passing the specified checks. The timing includes solver creation, setup, and solve, but excludes discovery, compilation, and independent verification. Selection used the development set. The released verification record adds a fresh correctness check, while performance on an unseen workload remains to be measured. Even with those limits, they demonstrate actual sparse-solver optimization. [32]

FrontierCS 2 brings that competition into recent research code. Its 4 November 18-system, 48-task snapshot includes 13 systems passing Clusterwise SpGEMM, eight passing the KAMI numerical-kernel task, and seven passing Polar Express. Each task uses its own correctness and performance criteria. They are nevertheless actual successes in sparse and numerical algorithm engineering. [69]

The SVG-EAR case reaches the proposed diagnostic role directly. Astra introduced a probe that periodically compares sparse attention with exact attention on a small sample of queries, then chooses a per-head budget from the measured error, with a dense fallback when no tested budget passes. It also supplied a variance correction absent from the reference method. The final implementation reduced mean runtime by about 18 percent, improved all three mean fidelity measures, and passed 43 of 48 hidden checks. The diagnostic and correction were not separately ablated, and a passed probe did not ensure every final fidelity check passed. Its five fidelity failures also occurred in a separate exact-attention implementation, so they cannot simply be assigned to sparse approximation. The agent nevertheless performed the sequence of measurement, diagnosis, and adaptive repair. [70]

ADSD provides a more targeted comparison. Across nine model/task comparisons, direct revision worsens error in five, whereas its combined diagnosis, literature-retrieval, and skill pipeline reports lower error throughout. Five rounds of direct revision on GOC-500 do not close the gap. This combined automated procedure can improve the tested searches beyond simply requesting another attempt. The ablation assesses diagnosis, retrieval, and skill construction together. Off-the-shelf solvers are excluded, and matching the final revision budget does not equalize the cost of constructing the pipeline. The result establishes a useful automated mechanism whose costs should be included in a comparison with specialist-led work. [30]

SciCode-Verified supplies a related comparison after correcting defective specifications and tests. On its 64 main problems, expert-written background raises GPT-5.5 from 65.6 to 90.6 percent, Gemini 3.5 Flash from 51.6 to 87.5 percent, and Claude Opus 4.8 from 57.8 to 78.1 percent. These are single-run results under the paper's two-environment OR grading rule. They measure the value of supplied background, not a live specialist's advantage or the agent's inability to produce the same information. The background sometimes supplies a derivation or prevents substitution of a familiar formulation for the stated one. Knowledge can matter greatly while becoming reusable once expressed. [35]

AlphaEvolve can search over constructors and algorithms and use multiple evaluation scores, including expensive accelerator measurements. TTT-Discover updates model weights while working on the current problem, using feedback from earlier attempts. Its reported method targets one high-performing result under a continuous reward, with applications in mathematical bounds, GPU kernels, and algorithm engineering. Transfer of the learned procedure to further problems is a separate question. A comparison should include agents that learn during the investigation. [44] [88]

Agents can improve their research machinery too. AIDE2 reports seven accepted changes during an eight-day autonomous run, with gains transferring to four held-out benchmarks. Its comparison with a human-engineered production agent concerns two agent systems. It establishes a mechanism for improving the research process. [55]

These cases establish that agents already perform parts of the proposed work and can alter the process used to discover methods.

8. Guarantees at the cost of the complete calculation

Evaluating an automated method requires tracing the preservation claim through the complete implementation. A learned component, its surrounding iteration, finite-precision arithmetic, and the final check can each change the conditions under which the method delivers its answer.

Learned components and the operator they produce

A research agent can discover a classical algorithm that uses no model when deployed. A neural component can predict a coarse space, a sparse correction, or a tuning decision. A surrogate can approximate a solution map. These are different mechanisms. For the finite programme, the central question is whether the resulting factor or hierarchy solves the original system at the required accuracy and total cost.

Sparse approximate inverses illustrate why representation design and hardware belong in the same argument. Yang and collaborators replace triangular applications with sparse matrix-vector products and train the admissible entries. Their comparisons change across matrix families and CPU/GPU implementations. Default multigrid remains strong on diffusion examples. The research decision concerns which representation preserves enough spectral information at the cost the hardware can actually deliver. [77]

Melchers, Dolean, and Abdelmalik compare four neural-operator coarse corrections while fixing the output coarse space. They vary input discretization and linear dependence on the right-hand side. Preserving right-hand-side linearity and matching the input projection to the output basis give the Neural Green's Operator a Galerkin-type coarse correction. The experiments connect these choices to spectral behaviour, stagnation, and compatibility with Krylov solvers. Their scope is specified two-dimensional problems and coarse bases, with most comparisons measuring iterations and a separate diffusion experiment measuring runtime. The contribution identifies structural relationships that guide the architecture of a learned component. [85]

Bao, Cui, and Li learn coarse spaces from errors left behind by smoothing. Their objective compares subspaces independently of the basis used to express them. A perturbation analysis relates the discrepancy, including pre- and postsmoothing, to the resulting two-grid operator. The sharper quantity has a small Gram-matrix representation, but forming it requires solves with the original matrix, so it is used offline. The convergence bounds are conditional and the experiments concern stated parameterized families. The work exposes a concrete next problem of finding an informative, affordable test for when to reuse, enrich, or rebuild the learned space. [86]

RAPNet learns sparse corrections to coarse and transfer operators within an algebraic multigrid hierarchy, with transfer from training subgraphs of about 4,000 nodes to million-node tests. For some graph families the training patches come from large graphs to expose relevant structures. Its setup comparison uses classical CPU execution and GPU RAPNet, counting transfers and inference. A general convergence guarantee for unrestricted corrections remains open. These results demonstrate meaningful transfer, with its scope set by the training graphs, hardware, and stated limitations. [37]

AutoSpec learns short spectral recurrences, producing matrix polynomials for approximation, preconditioning, and filtering. Its comparisons include Chebyshev preconditioners supplied with the same spectral estimates and show both wins and losses. The tested operators are positive definite after the stated transformations. Some originally indefinite eigenproblems are squared, and some difficult linear systems also use incomplete Cholesky. Those details are part of what the results mean. Learning can thus return a compact executable numerical procedure with recognizable operations. An iteration-count improvement still needs a separate account of probe cost, training, and application time before becoming an end-to-end speedup. [38]

Adaptation within a changing calculation is already a practical method. PCGBandit chooses among 33 preconditioner configurations during one OpenFOAM run without an offline training corpus. It counts learning and backstop costs. Its detailed analysis identifies costly early trials in Shercliff electric-potential solves, before enough evidence accumulates. The action set consists of 33 fixed configurations. Constructing a new coarse representation would extend that action space. This motivates better early diagnosis for workloads too short to amortize unsuccessful exploration. [62] He and collaborators also learn BoomerAMG hierarchy choices and within-solve relaxation online. Their 5,000-system sequences report total-runtime reductions of 36.1-43.3 percent for diffusion and 19.8-31.0 percent for diffusion-advection against the reference default. Repeated use can pay for information that is too expensive to acquire for one solve. [39]

These examples expose different costs of missing information. A learned coarse space can approximate useful directions while its effect on the iteration remains expensive to assess. An online selector can spend too much on early trials after a change. The next examples show how a compressed preconditioner can save memory while requiring more iterations. In each case the target is an affordable observation tied to the relevant error or convergence behaviour, followed by a decision whose cost the remaining calculation can repay. A policy for selecting among solvers can be assessed through independently checked outcomes. A diagnostic that certifies an answer also needs a guarantee controlling the failures it may miss.

Precision can change the mathematical construction

Fast algebra and numerical stability can also be reconciled. Demmel, Dumitriu, and Holtz give normwise-stable algorithms for standard dense linear-algebra operations with essentially the matrix-multiplication exponent, under their stated arithmetic and stability assumptions. That result neither preserves arbitrary sparse structure nor establishes practical constants. A sparse implementation still needs control of its structure, accuracy, and application cost. [60]

The OpenAI release also reports constructions with explicit implementation-cost accounting. OpenAI's manuscript on exact Fourier transforms reports an asymptotic operation bound below n log n while explicitly accounting for scalar preparation, schedule construction, and indexing. Its construction preserves working width and restores borrowed registers. The result assumes exact complex arithmetic, supplied roots of unity, and unrestricted coefficients. A floating-point realization would need its own stability analysis. The proposed architecture reaches well beyond a bare existence theorem. [56] A separate OpenAI manuscript on integer multiplication explicitly propagates fixed-point errors and bounds the final coefficient error below one half so that rounding recovers the exact answer. Its asymptotic bit-complexity result comes with enormous constants, not a useful practical crossover. These are reported constructions, not independently certified here. These reports put finite-precision analysis among the tasks to include in a comparison with research agents. [57]

Arithmetic precision is another resource to allocate. McCormick, Benzaken, and Tamstorf develop a purely algebraic mixed-precision multigrid analysis for sparse symmetric positive-definite systems. Residual evaluation, correction solves, and iterate updates contribute differently to the energy-error bound, and precision can vary across levels. This supplies an established framework for spending precision where it matters. Better adaptive rules must respect the convergence and rounding assumptions, as well as the costs of storing and applying the sparse operators. [59]

Jang, Jolivet, and Mary identify a structural failure in mixed-precision GMRES-DR. Its derivation uses simplified relations from Arnoldi iteration and orthogonality whose finite-precision defects can make the method stagnate. Returning to a more general augmented formulation permits lower-precision inner work without those simplifications. The experiments retain precision-sensitive cases and defer hardware-specific timing. The example links a diagnosis to a mathematical repair of the formulation. [63]

Pipelined conjugate gradients makes the pressure from execution especially explicit. It overlaps global communication with useful work and is equivalent to standard CG in exact arithmetic. Its additional recurrences can nevertheless amplify rounding errors, separating the recursively updated residual from the residual of the computed solution. Cools and collaborators analyze these coupled gaps and derive an estimate used in a practical heuristic for triggering selective recomputation of residuals and auxiliary quantities. Their experiments recover attainable accuracy while retaining efficient parallel performance. Unnecessary replacements cost extra matrix-vector products, so the diagnostic must justify when to act. The analysis targets attainable accuracy and leaves other effects, including loss of orthogonality, as further questions. Replacement can itself introduce rounding error or delay convergence. This is an established automatic repair, and a baseline for future specialist-agent work. [95]

A more focused question is whether the memory saved by compressing a preconditioner outweighs the cost of extra iterations. Khan and Carson report a steam1 case with 21 GMRES iterations against 14 in uniform precision, while storage falls to 74.9 or 42.6 percent of the uniform version under two thresholds. Those figures do not decide runtime: reduced traffic competes with extra iterations and other work. The authors leave predicting favorable matrices as further research. Meanwhile doubly adaptive GMRES already combines iteration-dependent and entry-dependent precision automatically, with an author-reported maximum 1.6-fold speedup. The research target is a better, transferable prediction of when these precision choices repay their cost. [64] [65]

A finite error bound that can be used in a computation

Suppose A and M are symmetric positive definite and a proved lower comparison gives α M ≤ A for α > 0. For an approximate solution y to A x = b, form the residual r = b - A y. In exact arithmetic the squared energy error has the following bound:

||x - y||A2 = rTA-1r ≤ (1/α) rTM-1r.

This follows by reversing the positive-definite matrix inequality under inversion. If M is inexpensive to solve with and α is useful, the bound turns a residual into an energy-error certificate. For the weight-change example above, use c α in place of α and recompute the residual against A'. The comparison obtained from edge weights can therefore support a check on the final answer. For Laplacians it applies on the subspace where the common kernel has been removed. Support theory is one established algebraic framework for bounding such comparisons. [52]

To use this bound, the lower comparison α must hold for the actual construction. Spectral probes require an argument connecting them to that bound. If M is solved only approximately, that error needs its own control. A rigorous floating-point certificate must also account for rounding in the residual and quadratic form. The cost of certification must be justified by the required assurance and the declared workload. A sharper bound or a cheaper valid check can itself be a research result. [21]

The shifted-Laplacian pilot also admits a conservative check independent of the preconditioner. Since A' ≥ μ I, any validated upper bound ρ on the Euclidean norm of r = b - A' y gives

||x - y||A′ ≤ ||r||2/√μ ≤ ρ/√μ.

Thus ρ/sqrt(μ) ≤ τ certifies a declared absolute energy-error tolerance τ. It requires no estimate of the hierarchy's quality. Residual evaluation and the norm bound still need rounding control. This separates accepting a computed answer from proving that a reused or updated preconditioner will reach it efficiently. The dependence on μ also makes the potential conservatism explicit. [21] [52]

The conservatism can itself be measured. For nonzero residual r and e = x - y, the ratio R between the exact-residual bound and the true energy error satisfies the identity below. On an eigenmode of A' with eigenvalue λ, R = sqrt(λ/μ). In the four-cycle's missed mode, λ = 3 ε and μ = ε, so R is exactly sqrt(3), independently of the contrast. Higher-energy directions can produce much larger overestimates. Using a validated upper bound ρ adds the separate factor ρ/||r||_2. Reference cases can reveal both sources of extra work.

A PDE application introduces an additional error between the continuous solution and the exact solution of the discrete system. Certified reduced-basis methods and adaptive finite-element estimators address that further layer under explicit assumptions. Yano's complementary-energy construction, for example, requires symmetric coercivity, affine parameter dependence, and specialized offline work. The p-robust adaptive analysis cited here has a computable contraction criterion and a stated Poisson setting. These are application-specific extensions, not prerequisites for studying the finite matrix algorithm. [27] [46]

Nonnormal matrices and affordable diagnostics

Crouzeix points to an adjacent programme in nonnormal matrix computation. For a function f holomorphic near the numerical range W(A), the reported resolution gives the following bound.

||f(A)||2 ≤ 2 maxz in W(A) |f(z)|.

For normal matrices the spectral theorem already supplies a constant-one bound. The new direction concerns operators for which eigenvalues alone give an incomplete account, including nonsymmetric systems and some directed-graph problems. Crouzeix and Greenbaum use the new result to improve bounds on other spectral regions and apply them to GMRES and rational Krylov methods. Choosing a useful region, approximation, and inner-solve accuracy is a further design problem. A numerical-range bound containing zero can fail to certify GMRES contraction. A valid cutout that excludes zero without surrounding it may help, but requires additional information about the inverse. [66]

The direction of the approximation matters when estimating a spectral region. For an orthonormal Krylov basis V, W(V* A V) lies inside W(A). This projected set is an inner approximation, while a certificate based on an outer enclosure needs a bound in the other direction. An ordinary largest Ritz value of a Hermitian support matrix is a lower estimate. An outer support line needs an upper bound. Chen and Urschel quantify Krylov approximation of numerical ranges and its limitations in exact arithmetic. A floating-point certificate requires further control. This identifies the information a cheap diagnostic must obtain before its estimate can support a rigorous guarantee. [67]

Verified sparse solving also has established automatic methods. Rump develops entrywise error bounds for real and complex sparse systems, using inexpensive bounds first and stronger residual-based work when needed. Different factorizations and fill patterns can change verification cost sharply. A new certificate must therefore improve an existing procedure, or apply where its assumptions fail, rather than claim that rigorous checking has not yet been automated. [68]

The common requirement is a representation together with the relationships that justify using it. A coarse space must represent errors left by its smoother. A learned correction must fit the surrounding iteration. An arithmetic shortcut must retain the identities on which its derivation depends, and a residual must control the requested error. Diagnosis is useful when it identifies which relationship has failed. A repair advances the computation when it restores enough control at a cost the workload can support. [20] [85] [63] [21]

Count the full cost and the failed cases

Performance claims need a stated workload and an account of failures. Chillón and collaborators' learned smoother makes the point sharply. On fine unstructured three-dimensional canonical-flow tests, the fraction of successful solves averages 67.7 percent across ten trained models. A separate evaluation selects the best complete-data model and tests it on 1,000 AirfRANS systems. It converges on 99.9 percent of those systems, uses about 27 percent fewer V-cycles, and gives only about a 4 percent solve-time speedup. The baseline is relaxed Jacobi, with relative tolerance 10-4 and a 500-cycle cap. [40]

The network predicts coefficients during setup. Applying the resulting sparse smoother costs more per cycle than diagonal Jacobi, absorbing much of the iteration saving. The two evaluations use different tests and aggregations. Together they show why iteration savings, model selection, and the cost of applying the learned operator must all be reported. They do not establish a general limit on learned smoothers. [40]

The distinction between a research agent and a solution surrogate also matters economically. Huang and Stuart's comparisons of neural operators with classical solvers depend on accuracy and on amortized training and data costs. The plateau they observe for particular Darcy surrogates is not evidence that an agent cannot discover a better sparse solver. For the present programme, the relevant comparison is the final matrix algorithm, its construction costs, and the workload over which those costs are recovered. [41]

Zhang, Li, and Saad test a learned multilevel preconditioner on 867 real non-SPD SuiteSparse matrices. Median per-matrix setup/training time is 135.77 seconds against 0.18 for the tested AMG. Median solve time is 8.08 against 1.31 seconds. These separate medians cannot be added to obtain a paired comparison of total time. The paper also reports useful cross-matrix transfer. Its convergence win shares are conditional on compared methods producing valid runs. Construction reliability, solution accuracy, and convergence speed are different outcomes, and a method intended for one solve faces different economics from a reusable trained construction. [42]

Assisted optimization and validation

An adjacent GPU study compares final kernels produced by assisted and autonomous development. Shui and colleagues evaluate selected kernels on matched B200 workloads and timing. Across five operator definitions, the assisted artifacts report lower mean latency, with autonomous-to-assisted ratios of 1.35-13.25. Human input includes reference selection, profiler interpretation, and changes of implementation route. Development budgets, model choices, and supplied context are not matched, so these artifact gains do not isolate the value of human guidance. [102]

The fastest reported top-k indexer also exposes the acceptance problem. It passes a three-trial check but fails three of 200 trials on a targeted workload. An earlier fallback passes 1,206 targeted trials. The other four definitions retain reported latency advantages. Full-workload validation, numerical edge cases, and the cost of finding and validating a fallback therefore belong in the comparison alongside speed. [102]

For a fixed workload of N comparable queries, include setup, recurring diagnosis, the initial solve attempt, checking, and expected recovery in the time model. Recovery includes additional failed attempts and the specified fallback. Fix timeout and fallback rules before estimating these costs, and report the fraction of queries that remain unresolved. An average restricted to successful runs omits part of the workload.

T(N) = Tsetup + N (Tdiagnose + Tsolve + Tcheck + E[Trecovery]).

On this expected-time model, a method with extra setup cost ΔS and a net saving d > 0 per query reaches expected break-even at N ≥ ΔS/d. Rebuilding after a coefficient change and correlated failures affect the costs and risks. Report their distribution alongside the mean. Discovery has its own accounting. Charge the research, training, validation, and transfer costs over the declared family of later uses, allowing every competitor the same amortization horizon. A costly investigation can produce an inexpensive method used millions of times, while a short deployment may justify very little exploratory work.

The checker must evaluate the supplied problem independently of the submitted answer. For a matrix task, fix the operator, right-hand side, gauge, and required components before running the candidate. Reject nonfinite outputs on those components. The FEniCSx scorer illustrates the importance of that independence. Its joint NaN-exclusion mask can omit points where a prediction is NaN, although missing reference values can legitimately indicate locations outside a physical domain. The checked domain and the treatment of missing reference values must therefore be defined independently of the candidate, with coverage reported. [33]

These cases sharpen the proposed division of work. The proposed specialist contribution is a discriminating test or diagnosis that redirects an agent's investigation at lower total cost. That cost includes producing the intervention, checking it, and recovering from a wrong diagnosis. Shared methods give every group a common starting point for deciding what the new instance requires. The following comparison would test whether specialist participation improves that decision and the resulting method. [20] [48] [63] [86] [95]

Short workloads sharpen the value of choosing correctly before paying for several solver trials. They also limit what can be spent obtaining that choice. The main opportunity I would pursue is discovering a diagnostic or repair rule that transfers across a declared family and is inexpensive to apply. Its discovery cost must be recovered over that family of uses. Human intervention during each individual short solve faces its own, stricter cost test. Once the rule is shared, the next evaluation must measure what further specialist contribution improves the investigation. [48] [62]

First assess what a construction delivers at the required accuracy and total cost on its stated workload. Test transfer on withheld families and assess a claimed theorem under its stated assumptions. These are distinct accomplishments. A uniform bound can clarify a method while leaving its computational economy unresolved. The following pilot gives the proposed programme a concrete test.

A first pilot can use shifted grid Laplacians A(w) = L(w) + μ I, with positive edge weights and fixed μ > 0. Build an initial hierarchy with one fixed multigrid implementation. Each trial starts from that initial operator and hierarchy, then makes one weight change and compares three policies. Weight-ratio certificates always refer to the initial weights. In every policy the outer iteration solves the changed system A' x = b. [76] [78] [79]

This first pilot tests economical selection among established actions. The elimination and coarse-space projects below also admit new representations, repair rules, and guarantees. Each project must assess the particular improvement it produces. A new selection rule can itself be a research result. The comparative discovery study needs multiple independently specified research tasks, with matrix realizations and random seeds treated as observations within those tasks.

Form their right-hand sides consistently and account for construction error. Normalization gives the prescribed absolute energy tolerance a comparable scale across the controlled tests. If the trajectories diverge, attribute the calibration to its own trajectory. Keep the deployed acceptance checker active in timed runs and charge it to solution cost. Charge probes, transfers of data, updates, coarse factorizations, setup, solves, independent checks, failed attempts, and fallback. On failed acceptance, rebuild and retry within a fixed budget; report any remaining unresolved case. Before evaluation, fix the worthwhile cost-improvement margin, uncertainty rule, and allowable failure rate. Report paired total times and outcome distributions at the same accuracy, so a benefit over a long reuse horizon cannot stand in for success on four solves.

Reserve graph families as well as seeds, including weakly joined clusters, long narrow connections, graded weights, degree variation, and larger instances. Report fill, peak memory, setup, factor application, convergence, independent error checks, failures, and fallback costs. Separate a single unfamiliar matrix, a short sequence after a structural change, and a long related sequence. A diagnosis should predict which regime repays its cost. [26] [48] [62]

Broader coarse-space work should include adaptive smoothed aggregation, compatible-relaxation or bootstrap constructions, and hierarchy-reuse policies on a declared SPD family. Compare with online configuration methods and well-chosen fixed settings, not only library defaults. On manageable sizes include an expensive spectral reference. Charge candidate generation, probes, local eigensolves, rejected attempts, rebuilding, and checking. Measure coarse dimension, transfer nonzeros, hierarchy size, memory, and total time. All groups may change support or aggregation and invent alternatives. An exact-coarse-solve theorem must be assessed separately from its recursive implementation. [20] [39] [54] [76] [78] [79]

A high-probability theorem must state its input assumptions, randomization, and failure-probability dependence. Practical predictions also require the relevant constants. Keep exact small-instance calculations, large-instance estimates, and validated proofs distinct. Independently recompute residuals against the original operator and check the declared kernel and positivity assumptions.

Across these projects, retain final evaluation cases outside development and record exploratory discoveries separately. Assess a proposed checker correction against the original mathematical requirement. An added application requirement defines a further task. [45]

What a specialist adds

To measure a specialist contribution, compare an autonomous agent, a specialist using that agent, and a mathematically capable generalist using it. Give them the same matrix interface, grounding convention, accuracy requirement, baseline source, development cases, and papers. All may change representations, prove lemmas, construct adversarial inputs, and improve their search procedures. Record human time, model use, computation, checking, and failed attempts. Use several participants and repeated seeds, with independent final assessment. Assess relevant competence before the experiment, fix participant eligibility and model and harness versions, and declare the primary budget-accounting rule. Report equal-time and equal-cost comparisons.

Before each evaluation round, define the primary outcome for the computational-discovery comparison as the probability of producing, within the declared research budget, a method that independently passes the accuracy and reliability requirements and delivers the prespecified worthwhile cost improvement on the evaluation workload. Distinguish that method-acceptance threshold from δ, the minimum worthwhile increase in discovery success probability. Estimate the specialist's advantage over the autonomous agent and over the generalist-assisted agent separately. Prespecify independent acceptance criteria for theorems, valid obstructions, and justified stopping decisions as additional outcomes. Record avoided downstream costs where the comparison supports them. A failed improved-method search alone leaves these other contributions unresolved.

Report person-hours, elapsed time, model expenditure, computation, and deployment costs separately. For combined economic comparisons, prespecify a human-hour price and the number of deployments over which discovery costs are amortized, then report sensitivity across declared alternative prices and horizons. Define the sampled projects or problem families and the population to which the comparison applies. Account for paired project comparisons, repeated participation, and multiple runs within a project. Additional seeds improve estimates of within-project variation. Claims across projects require sampling at that level. Before execution, fix the allocation scheme, independent project count, and a precision or power analysis for the planned effects. Use pilot information to choose these quantities while keeping final evaluation outcomes reserved.

Ju and collaborators report a useful precedent in structural algebra. From the same formalization checkpoint, Archon had three remaining unique-factorization obligations. The branch given a human proof blueprint finished in 2 hours 12 minutes, compared with 3 hours 43 minutes for the autonomous branch. The agent retained its own proof route while using intermediate observations from the blueprint. This is one reported comparison, with blueprint preparation uncharged and no generalist arm. It demonstrates how an intervention can be isolated, while leaving its full economic value and repeatability unresolved. [90]

Human participation alone does not establish independent judgment. In a randomized study, fifty professional analysts using agents followed deliberately assigned agent preferences on 86 percent of four analytical choices. The alternatives were defensible, so this is influence evidence rather than an accuracy ranking. In a separate mechanism comparison, record a participant's initial diagnosis before revealing the agent's recommendation, retain the proposed test, and measure whether the intervention improves the final evidence. Keep that additional constraint distinct from the natural collaboration arm. An approving signature is not the contribution being claimed. [74]

Allow every group to improve its search procedure, diagnostic tools, and evaluation harness within the same budget. Specify in advance how decision points will be selected for intervention tests, and retain unsuccessful interventions. At a selected checkpoint, freeze the solver, evidence, agent context, and any learned model state before introducing the proposed diagnosis or repair. Compare repeated continuations with and without that intervention under equal remaining compute budgets, charging its production cost to the group that supplied it and allowing the controls to generate their own alternatives. Record the diagnosis and its prediction before opening the reserved cases. These continuations estimate what the intervention changes at those particular points. The full-project comparison, including the cost of reaching those states and producing the intervention, tests the broader specialist advantage.

Test the proposed mechanism as well as the average result. Before seeing outcomes, classify cases by the stated representation change, the information available in cheap diagnostics, and the cost of further experiments. The hypothesis predicts a larger specialist contribution on ambiguous, costly diagnosis cases than on routine implementation cases. Record whether the intervention improves the predicted failure mechanism, choice of test, or predicted scope of the repair before it improves the final outcome. Give every group the same additional diagnostic information in a further declared comparison. A reduced specialist premium after that information is supplied would support the diagnostic account. It could also arise because the added information creates a success ceiling or compensates for differences in implementation or orchestration. Randomize the information condition and prespecify checks for these alternatives, using independently scored intermediate predictions and an appropriate information control. The complete structural projects should be assessed for these same features.

After each completed round, add useful procedures, counterexamples, and diagnostic rules to a shared archive before testing fresh cases. Measure whether making an artifact available improves subsequent verified work, including retrieval and application costs. Separately ask whether a specialist still adds value once all groups have that equipment. Distinguish the cost of discovering a useful intervention from the gains after it becomes available to everyone. Freeze the shared archive before each round and keep that round's reserved results out of it until assessment is complete.

Accumulation alone does not establish useful reuse. Berlot-Attwell and collaborators re-examine three LLM library-learning systems and find that claimed advantages often disappear or reverse against prompting baselines with comparable computational resources. Their detailed analysis finds no direct reuse of LEGO-Prover's learned lemmas and evidence against the proposed indirect mechanism. These findings concern the studied systems. They motivate a behavioural test of whether an acquired abstraction or procedure actually improves later work enough to repay its cost. [89]

Fix the sample size and stopping rule, or use a prespecified sequential procedure with valid coverage under repeated inspection. Use confidence bounds that account for the two specialist comparisons and the dependence in the design. For either contrast, a lower bound above δ supports a worthwhile specialist premium. An upper bound below δ supports setting that premium aside for the tested scope, budgets, and agent versions. An interval spanning δ leaves the claim unresolved. A nonsignificant difference from zero alone cannot settle it. If specialist assistance improves on autonomy but the generalist comparison rules out a worthwhile additional specialist gain, the supported conclusion is effective assistance without an additional specialization premium of the declared size. New agent versions can motivate a further declared round while retaining the completed comparison's result. [92]

Repeat the comparison as agents improve, keeping the application scope and acceptance rules fixed and using fresh cases selected before the comparative results are known. A continuing specialist premium must survive both better agents and shared discoveries. The numerical programme succeeds when it yields reliable methods whose benefits repay their costs, including methods that agents later discover or use unaided.

A ranking of specialties would require corresponding complete structural projects, including definition and conjecture formation, imported theory, counterexamples, proof construction, and independent assessment. The proposed experiment answers the narrower question of what works on its declared numerical workload and who improves its discovery. For the structural comparison, prespecify an independently assessable mathematical deliverable and its acceptance criterion. Compare the specialist-induced increase in the probability of attaining that deliverable against the corresponding autonomous baseline. For the separate matched analysis, define criteria before selecting evaluation projects, including novelty, available theory, and the cost and informativeness of feedback. Calibrate baseline difficulty on separate development projects to address ceiling and floor effects within those matched project classes. For a comparative conclusion, estimate the difference between the numerical and structural specialist premiums and its uncertainty under the declared budget and matching rules. A significant effect in one portfolio and a nonsignificant effect in the other do not establish a difference between them.

The portfolio and mechanism comparisons answer different questions. For the allocation claim, define the candidate-project sampling frame for each portfolio before seeing results and retain its project mix, including the frequency of costly, ambiguous diagnostic situations. A separate stratified or matched analysis can examine whether those features predict the specialist increment. Matching on the proposed explanatory features conditions away their prevalence difference. Conclusions about the selected portfolios and conclusions within matched project classes should therefore be reported separately. Remaining differences after matching observed features can still reflect unmeasured project differences.

Write D(b) for the numerical specialist premium minus the structural specialist premium at budget b. Include this contrast in the declared confidence procedure, accounting jointly for project dependence, the other planned comparisons, and any repeated inspection. Specify a comparative margin η, with zero for a directional claim or a positive value for a minimum worthwhile difference. A lower confidence bound above η supports the comparative claim. An upper bound below η sets aside that size of comparative advantage for the tested scope, and an interval spanning η leaves it unresolved. A positive numerical-centaur recommendation also requires a worthwhile numerical specialist premium. A positive D alone can arise when guidance harms both portfolios but harms the structural one more. Claims that specialization contributes more in one portfolio also require comparing the specialist-minus-generalist gains across portfolios, with a declared comparative margin and joint uncertainty.

These comparisons measure complementarity for the declared portfolios, acceptance targets, budgets, participants, and agent versions. Scientific or economic comparisons of the resulting deliverables require additional valuations. The experiment uses existing expertise. Assessing the return on acquiring that expertise over years also requires training costs, alternative paths, and assumptions about future agents. Prespecified additional budgets can test sensitivity, but a run still unsuccessful when stopped at one budget does not establish whether continued investigation would succeed at a larger budget.

10. Where to concentrate research effort

In choosing a programme of mathematical research, I regard the strategy of seeking protection in deeper abstraction as spent. Algebraic geometry, representation theory, abstract algebra, and theoretical PDE offer no exemption because their objects are remote or their arguments sophisticated. The demonstrated machine contributions reach the conceptual level invoked to justify that exemption.

My positive recommendation is to invest specialist-AI work in adaptive sparse computation, where graph structure, numerical error, and execution cost must be controlled together. The strongest reported machine constructions already include invariant preservation, interface repair, and costed adaptive work. The remaining targets must be equally specific. A local sampling bound can leave downstream fill uncontrolled. A useful coarse-space discrepancy can cost too much to evaluate. Changed coefficients can defeat an old coarse space despite unchanged familiar matrix summaries. A communication-saving recurrence can acquire new rounding-error obligations. These are recurring mathematical decisions about economical, reliable computation, with demonstrated consequences for implemented methods. [48] [86] [95] [97] [98]

Progress may take the form of a bound, an obstruction, a construction, or a diagnostic. Its computational value depends on whether the intended workload repays discovery, construction, execution, checking, and recovery. I would prioritize the selected adaptive-sparse portfolio for specialist-AI research because it offers consequential, observable decisions through which a compact mathematical intervention could redirect substantial implementation and experimental work. Documented scientific-software interventions and assisted numerical development strengthen that mechanism. The comparison is with capable structural teams developing statements, representations, proofs, and reusable infrastructure. My prediction concerns which collaboration produces the larger increase in accepted discoveries within budget. The present evidence supports concrete numerical opportunities and particular interventions, while the proposed portfolio comparison must establish the relative gain. Successful methods must be shared before the next round measures what additional contribution remains. This keeps the positive thesis attached to mathematical and computational progress, while exposing the proposed human advantage to a test it can fail. [101] [102]

The condition number is 2, exceeding the claimed bound sqrt(5)/2, and the claimed lower bound 1 fails. Write sigma_hat_l for the smallest retained singular value of the CUR approximation. The regularized default in Section 5.2 meets the added target restriction. Every phase still requires its approximation-error condition. Other positive targets admit different bounds. This corrects the printed guarantee without re-evaluating the experimental results.↩
