The mechanism

Turn local oscillation into finite control

For the sufficiency direction of Lebesgue’s criterion, the proof groups points by the amount a bounded function can oscillate near them. Covering the exceptional set and using compactness elsewhere turns local information into a finite partition with a small Darboux gap.

What becomes elementary: A pointwise continuity condition becomes a quantitative estimate on upper and lower sums.

Follow the passage from oscillation to a finite cover and then to the partition estimate. Compare the same local-to-global operation in the product proof.

Overview

A bounded function whose discontinuities form a set of measure zero is Riemann integrable. We prove this by covering the discontinuities with intervals of small total length and using continuity on the complement to control oscillation. A finite subcover yields a partition for which every term in the Darboux gap is controlled by one of these two estimates. The construction keeps track of cell endpoints, where bounds on open intervals alone would not suffice. A final application uses the same division of the Darboux sum to prove stability under continuous composition.

2 · Two ways to control the same sum

Each term of the Darboux gap is a product of an oscillation and an interval length. The proof uses this in two complementary ways. On the cells indexed by , continuity controls the oscillation while their total length is at most . On those indexed by , boundedness controls the oscillation while the measure-zero hypothesis makes their total length small. Assigning each cell to exactly one class makes the estimates compatible even when the covering intervals overlap.

The argument depends on the total length of a cover, not on how densely the discontinuities are distributed. Its discontinuity set is therefore dense and countable, but has measure zero, so the theorem applies.

Uncountable discontinuity sets are also possible. The indicator of the middle-thirds Cantor set on is discontinuous exactly on that set: it is locally constant on the complement, while every neighborhood of a point in the set meets the complement. Since the Cantor set has measure zero, its indicator is Riemann integrable as well.
