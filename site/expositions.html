Matrices and graphs

Separate a recurrence, count spanning trees, and prove a matrix identity. A first example →

  • Find the quantity that moves on its own

    11 min

    Choose a weighted difference, derive the weight, and turn a coupled recurrence into a single update.

    Background: Algebra and a second-order recurrence.

  • A determinant that counts trees

    13 min

    Build the incidence matrix and watch its minors select exactly the spanning trees.

    Background: Matrices and determinants; Cauchy–Binet is stated and applied.

  • A matrix identity by cycle exchange

    Cayley–Hamilton · 22 min

    Augment walks with reserved cycles, then pair terms with equal edge products and opposite signs to prove Cayley–Hamilton.

    Background: Matrix multiplication and the permutation definition of a determinant.

  • Classify compatible couplings

    14 min

    Multiply block matrices, derive the compatibility map and the allowed gauge map, then row-reduce.

    Background: Block matrices and solving linear equations over a field.

  • Construct every tensor chain

    18 min

    Build a lowering operator, solve its coefficient recurrence, and construct chains of every required length.

    Background: Finite-dimensional linear algebra and polynomial arithmetic; characteristic zero is explicit.

  • Cancel the unwanted edge sets

    12 min

    Choose the least available edge and pair terms by an involution that reverses their signs.

    Background: Finite graphs, subsets, and inclusion–exclusion.

Notes and problems

Further arguments in algebra, arithmetic, and analysis. The full texts and downloads are linked from each page.

Algebra & geometry

  • When a Formal Logarithm Fails

    Change coordinates one degree at a time: a quadratic repair succeeds, but a cubic obstruction remains in characteristic three. A filtered complex identifies the precise failure.

    Background: Polynomial expansion and characteristic-three arithmetic suffice for the first complete calculation in §1.

  • Divisibility of Separated Polynomials

    A free-module expansion and a diagonal remainder calculation expose when divisibility of polynomial differences forces composition through a common polynomial.

    Background: Polynomial rings and basic field theory.

  • When a nonlinear law becomes additive

    Determine exactly when a parameterized multiplication law becomes addition, construct the coordinate and its inverse, and classify the cubic term retained by every coordinate beginning with t.

    Background: Polynomial multiplication and arithmetic modulo a prime.

Number theory

  • IBM Ponder This July 2010

    Collect algebraic conjugates into an integer trace recurrence, then use a companion matrix to turn the rounding question into finite modular arithmetic.

    Background: Algebraic numbers, polynomial roots, and modular arithmetic.

  • Extracting the First Prime Greater than m

    Build a finite arithmetic selector for the first prime above a bound, using factorial congruences, greatest common divisors, and valuations.

    Background: Elementary number theory, factorials, and divisibility.

Analysis & topology

  • A Weighting Argument for Pointwise Convergence to Convergence in Measure

    Choose a positive measurable weight that changes the scale at each point, turning almost-everywhere convergence into convergence in measure for the weighted sequence.

    Background: Measure theory and convergence of measurable functions.

  • A Note on Disjoint Decompositions of [0,1) into Closed Sets

    A carefully chosen closed remainder exposes the Baire-category obstruction to a countable partition of [0,1) into sets closed in the real line.

    Background: Closed sets and the Baire category theorem.

  • Lebesgue's Criterion for Riemann Integrability

    Prove the sufficiency direction of Lebesgue’s criterion: local oscillation and a small discontinuity set give finite control of a Darboux sum.

    Background: Riemann integration, compactness, and sets of measure zero.

  • Products Preserve Riemann Integrability

    Insert one mixed term into a product difference, bound the separate oscillations, and sum over a common partition to preserve Riemann integrability.

    Background: Bounded functions, upper and lower Darboux sums, and partition refinement.

Problems

  • Selected Problems in Discrete Mathematics and Analysis

    Six problems invite different choices of what to count, track, or record: congruences, recurrences, series, and observation rules. Statements are supplied without solutions.

    Background: Counting, elementary number theory, recurrences, and convergence of series.
